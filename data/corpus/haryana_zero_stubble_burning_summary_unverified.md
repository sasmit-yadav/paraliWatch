# Haryana measures for zero stubble burning: summary provided by the project author (NOT YET VERIFIED against official notices)

The Haryana Government has implemented a multi-pronged strategy to achieve Zero Stubble Burning by 2030, combining financial incentives, machinery subsidies, and strict enforcement. Key initiatives include the Parali Protsahan Yojana, Mera Pani Meri Virasat Yojana, and the Rajya Vishisht Yojana, supported by a dedicated Parali Protection Force for monitoring.

## Financial incentives for farmers

Stubble management: Farmers receive Rs 1,000 to Rs 1,200 per acre for managing crop residue without burning via the Parali Protsahan Yojana or Crop Residue Management (CRM) scheme.

Crop diversification: The Mera Pani Meri Virasat Yojana offers an enhanced incentive of Rs 8,000 per acre to farmers who switch from water-intensive paddy to alternative crops.

DSR adoption: Farmers adopting the Direct Seeded Rice (DSR) technique receive Rs 4,500 per acre.

Biomass delivery: An additional Rs 500 per metric tonne is provided for delivering paddy straw to the 2G Ethanol Plant in Panipat.

## Machinery subsidies and infrastructure

Subsidies: The government provides a 50% subsidy on CRM machines (for example Super Seeder, Happy Seeder, Balers) for individual farmers and an 80% subsidy for Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs).

Deployment: The state has distributed over 90,000 machines and plans to supply an additional 10,588 super seeders and 1,405 baling units for in-situ and ex-situ management.

Storage: Government and panchayat land is allocated for straw bale storage, with supply chains mapped to connect farmers with industries for pelletization and briquetting.

## Enforcement and community rewards

Penalties: Violations result in FIR registration, fines ranging from Rs 5,000 to Rs 30,000, and a red entry in the farmer's land record, which blocks access to e-kharid crop sales for two seasons.

Panchayat awards: Villages are classified into red, yellow and green zones; panchayats in red and yellow zones receive awards of Rs 1 lakh and Rs 50,000 respectively for achieving zero burning cases.

Monitoring: A Parali Protection Force comprising police and agricultural officials, along with drone surveillance via the DRIISHYA system, ensures strict implementation of the action plan.
